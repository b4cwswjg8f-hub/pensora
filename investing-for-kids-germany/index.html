Investment Accounts for Kids in Germany

Custodial depot accounts, the child's own tax allowance, and the new government-funded Frühstart-Rente — how to start building wealth for your kids.

Starting an investing habit for your kids while they're young gives compound interest decades to work — and in Germany, each child has their own separate tax allowance, which makes custodial investing meaningfully more tax-efficient than simply investing more in a parent's own name.

Custodial (Junior) accounts

A Minderjährigendepot is a brokerage account opened in the child's name, managed by the parent(s) as legal custodians until the child turns 18. Gains and dividends are taxed against the child's own Sparer-Pauschbetrag and personal tax allowances — often meaning little to no tax at all on modest accounts.

The tradeoff: at 18, legal control passes fully to the child. Parents can't restrict or override how they use the money from that point — worth factoring into how much you build up and how you talk to your kids about it as they approach adulthood.

The Frühstart-Rente

Starting in 2026 (phased in by birth year), the German government will pay €10 a month into an individual retirement investment account for every child aged 6 to 18 — automatically, with no application needed and regardless of family income. Parents, grandparents and godparents can add their own contributions on top.

Gifts and larger contributions

Beyond regular small contributions, larger one-time gifts into a child's account can trigger German gift tax (Schenkungsteuer) rules — which mirror the inheritance tax framework, including a €400,000 allowance per parent that resets roughly every 10 years. Worth planning deliberately if you're thinking about a significant lump sum.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I open a custodial investment account for my child in Germany?
Yes. Most German and EU brokers offer a custodial account (Minderjährigendepot / "Junior-Depot") opened in the child's name and managed by the parents until the child turns 18, when full control transfers to them.
Does my child have their own tax-free allowance?
Yes. Each child has their own Sparer-Pauschbetrag (€1,000/year as of 2025) and their own basic income tax allowance, separate from their parents'.
What is the Frühstart-Rente?
A new German government program starting in 2026 (phased in by birth year): the state pays €10/month into an individual retirement investment account for every child from age 6 to 18, automatically and regardless of family income.
What happens to a custodial account when my child turns 18?
Legal control transfers entirely to the child at 18. Parents can no longer make decisions about the account, and the child can withdraw or use the funds however they choose.
Can grandparents or godparents contribute too?
Yes, anyone can contribute or gift money toward a child's account. Larger one-time gifts may be subject to German gift tax rules and allowances (Schenkungsteuer).
