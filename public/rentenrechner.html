Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the average pension in Germany in 2025?
The average statutory pension in 2025 is roughly €1,200–1,500/month gross for men and €900–1,100 for women (western Germany). After tax and health-insurance contributions, net amounts are typically €900–1,300, depending on the taxable share.
What is the 2025 pension value (Rentenwert)?
The current pension value (equalized east/west) is €40.79 per earnings point as of 1 July 2025. It's expected to rise to €42.52 (+4.24%) from July 2026.
How many earnings points do I need for a €1,000 pension?
At a pension value of €40.79: €1,000 / €40.79 = approx. 24.5 earnings points. At an average income, that takes about 24.5 working years.
How is the pension taxed?
The taxable share rises each year: 85% in 2025, 87.5% by 2030, 100% by 2040. A fixed share applies based on your retirement year. There's also a flat deduction of €1,230/year for expenses.
From when can I retire?
The standard retirement age in 2025 is 67 (for birth years from 1964). Early retirement from 63 is possible with a 0.3% reduction per month before the standard age. Long-term contributors (45 years of contributions) can retire earlier without deductions.
Do years worked abroad count toward my German pension?
It depends on your home country and any social security agreement Germany has with it. Within the EU/EEA and countries with a totalization agreement, contribution periods are often aggregated for eligibility, though the pension amount is still based on German contributions. Check your specific situation with the Deutsche Rentenversicherung.
