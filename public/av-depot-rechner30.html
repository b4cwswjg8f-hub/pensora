Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Altersvorsorgedepot and when does it start?
The Altersvorsorgedepot is the legally approved successor to the Riester pension. The Bundestag passed the reform on 27 March 2026, the Bundesrat approved it on 8 May 2026. From 1 January 2027, providers can offer Altersvorsorgedepot accounts. New Riester contracts will no longer be possible; existing ones continue.
How much is the government's base bonus?
Up to €540 per year, tiered: 50% bonus on the first €360 of your own contribution (= €180), then 25% on contributions up to €1,800/year (= up to another €360). Contributing €150/month gets you the full €540 bonus.
How much is the child bonus?
Up to €300 per year per child you receive Kindergeld for. The full bonus is reached from just €25/month contributed. A family with two children can get up to €600 in child bonus plus up to €540 in base bonus — up to €1,140/year in total support.
What is the young-professional bonus?
A one-time €200 bonus for opening an account before your 25th birthday, on top of the base bonus — meant to reward starting early, when compound interest has the most time to work.
Can expats and foreign residents use it?
Yes — eligibility is based on being taxable in Germany, not citizenship. If you pay German income tax, you're generally eligible, making it one of the most accessible subsidized savings vehicles for expats.
How is it different from Riester?
No more mandatory contribution guarantee — the new system allows up to 100% equity allocation, unlike Riester's return-limiting capital guarantee. The base bonus is also three times higher: €540/year vs. €175/year under Riester.
When can I withdraw the money?
No earlier than age 65, and payouts must begin by age 70. Early withdrawal before retirement generally isn't possible — otherwise all bonuses received must be repaid.
Is it better than a regular ETF account?
It depends on your situation. Advantages: up to €540/year in bonuses, up to €300/child, tax-free growth while saving. Disadvantages: capital is locked until retirement, a limited range of securities, and full income-tax on payout instead of the 25% flat capital-gains rate. For families with children and lower earners, it's often clearly the better choice.
