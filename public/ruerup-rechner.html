Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Rürup pension (Basisrente)?
The Rürup pension is a government-subsidized retirement product for the self-employed and employees. Contributions have been 100% tax-deductible as special expenses since 2023. Payout is lifelong, starting no earlier than age 62 (for contracts from 2012), and cannot be taken as a lump sum.
How big is the Rürup tax benefit in 2025?
Maximum contribution 2025: €29,344 (single) / €58,688 (married couple). At a 40% marginal tax rate and €300/month contribution (€3,600/year): tax saving = €3,600 × 40% = €1,440/year = €120/month. Net cost is only €180/month instead of €300.
What is an ETF-based Rürup plan?
With an ETF-based Rürup plan, the provider invests your contributions in ETFs instead of classic life-insurance products. You benefit from potentially higher returns (historically 5–7% after costs) with the same tax benefit. Compare providers with an independent financial advisor.
How is the Rürup pension taxed in retirement?
In retirement, the taxable share under § 22 EStG applies: 85% in 2025, rising 0.5% per year to 100% by 2040. In other words: someone retiring in 2040 pays income tax on 100% of their Rürup pension — but their contributions were fully tax-deductible.
Does Rürup make sense for the self-employed and expats?
Self-employed people don't pay into the statutory pension system, so they have the full Rürup contribution room available. At a marginal tax rate above ~30% and a long investment horizon, Rürup is often worthwhile — including for expats who are self-employed or freelancing in Germany.
