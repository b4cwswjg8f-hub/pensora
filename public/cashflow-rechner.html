Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 50/15/15/10/10 rule?
A rule of thumb for splitting your income: 50% fixed costs (rent, groceries, transport), 15% retirement savings (Rürup, ETFs, employer pension), 15% leisure and lifestyle, 10% insurance, and 10% wealth-building (ETF savings plan, cash reserve). We compare your actual numbers against these benchmarks.
How big should my emergency fund be?
Financial planners recommend 3 months of net salary as a liquid reserve in an instant-access savings account (currently around 3% p.a.). At €3,000 net income, that's €9,000. Only once this buffer is in place should you invest for the long term.
How much should I save for retirement?
As a minimum: 10–15% of net income. At €3,000 net, that's €300–450/month. Including employer contributions or tax benefits (e.g. from a Rürup plan), your effective out-of-pocket amount can be significantly lower.
What should I do with money left over each month?
Invest 60–70% of what's left after all expenses into retirement savings or investing, and keep 30–40% as a liquidity buffer. A concrete plan is best worked out in a consultation.
