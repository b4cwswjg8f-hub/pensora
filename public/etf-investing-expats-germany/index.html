ETF Investing for Expats in Germany

Opening a broker account, understanding Abgeltungssteuer and Teilfreistellung, and building an ETF savings plan — the basics for anyone new to investing in Germany.

Moving to Germany often means encountering an investing culture that looks different from what you're used to — savings accounts and life insurance are still far more common than brokerage accounts. That's changing, and as an expat you're not restricted from participating: anyone with a German tax ID can open a Depot (brokerage account) and start investing.

Opening a broker account

You'll need a Steuer-ID (issued automatically after registering your address, or Anmeldung) and, for most brokers, a German or EU bank account. Many online brokers now offer fully English-language onboarding and apps, and support automatic monthly ETF purchases (a Sparplan) from as little as €25/month.

How your gains get taxed

Germany taxes investment income at a flat 25% rate plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax — an effective rate of about 26.375%, regardless of your income tax bracket. This is usually withheld automatically by your German broker, so in most cases you don't need to declare it separately on your tax return.

Equity ETFs (funds holding at least 51% stocks) benefit from a 30% partial exemption (Teilfreistellung): only 70% of the gain is taxed. This exists to roughly offset corporate-level taxes the fund itself pays. Bond and money-market ETFs don't get this exemption.

Every person also gets a tax-free allowance (Sparer-Pauschbetrag) of €1,000 per year (€2,000 for married couples), which you can register with your broker via a Freistellungsauftrag so gains up to that amount aren't taxed at source.

What to actually invest in

This isn't investment advice, but the most common starting point for long-term investors in Germany is a single broad, low-cost, accumulating ETF tracking a global index like the MSCI World or FTSE All-World — simple, diversified, and easy to hold for decades via a savings plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can expats in Germany invest in ETFs?
Yes. Anyone with German residency and a tax ID (Steuer-ID) can open a brokerage account (Depot) with a German or EU broker and invest in ETFs, regardless of citizenship. Some brokers require a German address; most accept English-language onboarding.
How are ETF gains taxed in Germany?
Capital gains and dividends are subject to a flat 25% withholding tax (Abgeltungssteuer) plus a 5.5% solidarity surcharge on that tax, so about 26.375% effective. Equity ETFs (holding 51%+ stocks) get a 30% partial exemption (Teilfreistellung), reducing the effective taxable base.
What is the annual tax-free allowance for investment income?
The Sparer-Pauschbetrag is €1,000 per person per year (€2,000 for married couples filing jointly) as of 2025. You can file a Freistellungsauftrag with your broker so gains up to this amount aren't taxed at source.
Which broker should I use as an expat?
We don't recommend specific brokers (this isn't investment advice), but look for: English-language support, low or no custody fees, automatic German tax reporting, and a savings-plan feature for regular ETF purchases.
What is the Vorabpauschale?
An advance lump-sum tax charged annually on accumulating (non-distributing) ETFs, even if you haven't sold anything, based on a notional base rate of return. It's designed to prevent indefinite tax deferral and is credited against tax due when you eventually sell.
