RSUs & Stock Options: Taxation in Germany

Equity compensation is common at Stuttgart-region and Germany-wide tech employers — here's how RSUs and stock options actually get taxed.

If you're relocating to Germany with an equity package from a US or international tech employer, the tax treatment is meaningfully different from what many people expect — and getting the timing wrong can mean an unpleasant cashflow surprise.

RSUs are taxed at vesting, not at sale

The moment your RSUs vest, the full market value of the shares on that date counts as ordinary employment income — taxed at your personal income tax rate (up to 45%, plus the solidarity surcharge) and subject to standard German wage tax withholding, just like your salary. This is true even if you don't sell a single share.

Most employers handle the withholding automatically, often through a "sell-to-cover" arrangement that sells a portion of the newly vested shares to cover the tax bill. It's worth confirming exactly how your specific employer's plan handles this — the mechanics vary.

What happens later, when you sell

Once shares have vested and been taxed as income, any further gain (or loss) between that vesting-date value and your eventual sale price is treated as an ordinary capital gain — taxed at the flat 25% Abgeltungssteuer rate plus solidarity surcharge, not your income tax rate. This is a meaningfully lower rate than income tax for most earners, which is why holding vested shares (rather than selling immediately) can sometimes make sense from a pure tax perspective — though concentration risk is a separate consideration.

Stock options work differently

Classic stock options (not RSUs) are generally taxed at exercise: the spread between your strike price and the market value at the time you exercise counts as ordinary income. Some structured plans have partial deferral or exemption features, but these vary significantly by plan design and are worth reviewing individually.

Relocating mid-vesting

If some of your vesting period happened before you moved to Germany, the tax picture can get genuinely complicated — Germany may only tax the portion of the vesting period spent as a German resident, with credit for tax already paid elsewhere depending on the relevant treaty. This is exactly the kind of situation worth a proper individual review rather than guesswork.

Frequently Asked Questions

When are RSUs taxed in Germany?
At vesting, not at grant. The full market value of the shares on the vesting date is treated as ordinary employment income, taxed at your personal income tax rate and subject to normal wage tax withholding.
Does my employer withhold tax on RSUs automatically?
Usually yes — most employers withhold German wage tax on the vested value through payroll, often via a "sell-to-cover" mechanism. Confirm exactly how your employer handles this, as it varies.
What happens when I sell shares after vesting?
Any gain between the vesting-date value (already taxed as income) and your eventual sale price is a separate capital gain, taxed at the flat 25% Abgeltungssteuer rate — not your income tax rate.
How are stock options (not RSUs) taxed?
Typically at exercise: the spread between the strike price and the market value at exercise is taxed as ordinary income. Some structured plans have partial deferral rules — this varies by plan type.
What if I relocated to Germany mid-vesting?
Germany may tax only the portion of the vesting period spent as a German tax resident, apportioned against tax already paid elsewhere, depending on the relevant treaty. Worth reviewing individually.
