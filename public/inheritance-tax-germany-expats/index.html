Inheritance Tax in Germany for Expats

Erbschaftsteuer basics — tax classes, allowances, and why cross-border estates can get expensive fast if you don't plan ahead.

German inheritance tax (Erbschaftsteuer) is triggered by residency, not citizenship — and it can apply to your worldwide estate, not just what you own in Germany. That comes as a surprise to many expats who assume it's only relevant to German nationals or German property.

How the tax classes work

Germany splits heirs into three tax classes based on their relationship to the deceased, each with its own allowance and rate schedule:

RelationshipClassAllowance
SpouseI€500,000
Children / grandchildren (parent deceased)I€400,000
Grandchildren (parent alive)I€200,000
Siblings, nieces/nephews, in-lawsII€20,000
Unmarried partners, friends, othersIII€20,000

Rates then scale with the size of the inheritance and the tax class — from 7% at the low end for Class I up to 50% for large inheritances in Class III.

The unmarried-partner trap

This is where many expat couples get caught out: an unmarried partner, no matter how long the relationship, is treated the same as a stranger under German law — Class III, €20,000 allowance, rates starting at 30%. Marriage (or a registered partnership) is the single biggest lever for reducing this exposure.

Cross-border estates

If you or your heirs have ties to another country, that country may also claim the right to tax the same inheritance. Germany has inheritance-tax treaties with only a handful of countries. Without one, you're generally relying on each country's own unilateral relief rules — which don't always add up to full double-tax elimination. This is worth reviewing well before it becomes urgent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does German inheritance tax apply to expats?
Yes, in most cases. If either the person who died or the heir is a German tax resident at the time of death, German Erbschaftsteuer generally applies to the worldwide estate — not just German assets.
What are the German inheritance tax classes?
Steuerklasse I covers spouses, children and grandchildren. Steuerklasse II covers siblings, nieces/nephews and in-laws. Steuerklasse III covers everyone else, including unmarried partners, with the highest rates and lowest allowance.
What are the tax-free allowances?
A spouse can inherit €500,000 tax-free, children €400,000 per parent, grandchildren €200,000, and everyone else only €20,000. Allowances reset roughly every 10 years between the same two people.
Can double taxation happen with a foreign estate?
Yes, if your home country also taxes inheritances. Germany has inheritance-tax treaties with only a few countries; without one, you may rely on unilateral foreign tax credit rules, which don't always fully eliminate double taxation.
Does an unmarried partner get any allowance?
An unmarried partner falls into the least favorable Steuerklasse III, with only a €20,000 allowance and tax rates from 30% to 50% — a common and costly surprise for expat couples who haven't married or made a will.
