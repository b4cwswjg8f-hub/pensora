German Tax Residency for Expats

The 183-day rule, what "unlimited tax liability" actually means, and how double-tax treaties protect you from paying twice.

Tax residency determines not just what you owe, but where — and for expats splitting time or income across borders, getting it wrong can be expensive. German tax residency is based on facts, not intent: where you actually live and how long you actually spend in the country.

The two tests

You become a German tax resident if either applies: you maintain a permanent home available to you in Germany (Wohnsitz) — even if you're not there constantly — or you spend more than 183 days in Germany within a calendar year (gewöhnlicher Aufenthalt). Either one alone is enough to trigger residency; you don't need both.

What residency actually means

Once you're a German tax resident, you have "unlimited tax liability" (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht) — Germany taxes your worldwide income, not just what you earn inside the country. That includes foreign employment income, rental income, investment gains, and more, though how much of it Germany can actually collect depends on any applicable tax treaty.

Avoiding double taxation

Germany has double-tax treaties with most major economies. These allocate taxing rights between the two countries for different income types (employment, dividends, pensions, real estate, etc.) and provide relief through either an exemption method or a foreign tax credit, so the same euro of income generally isn't taxed in full twice.

It's entirely possible to meet the residency test in two countries at once under their respective domestic rules. Treaties include tie-breaker criteria — permanent home, center of vital interests, habitual abode, and nationality, in that order — to settle which country gets primary taxing rights when that happens.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes me a German tax resident?
You're generally a German tax resident if you have a permanent home available in Germany, or if you spend more than 183 days in Germany within a calendar year — either is enough on its own.
Does German tax residency mean my worldwide income is taxed?
Yes. German tax residents are taxed on their worldwide income (unbeschränkte Steuerpflicht), not just income earned in Germany. Non-residents are only taxed on German-source income.
What is a double-tax treaty and how does it help?
Germany has double-tax treaties with most major countries, allocating taxing rights between the two countries and providing relief via exemption or a tax credit, so the same income isn't fully taxed twice.
Can I be tax resident in two countries at once?
Yes, under each country's own domestic rules. Double-tax treaties include tie-breaker rules (permanent home, center of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality) to determine which country gets primary taxing rights.
Do I need to deregister when I leave Germany?
Yes — deregistering your address (Abmeldung) and informing your tax office are important steps. Failing to formally end your German tax residency can leave you exposed to continued German tax obligations.
