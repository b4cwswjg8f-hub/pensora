Buying Property in Germany as a Foreigner

Mortgage access for expats, the real cost of Grunderwerbsteuer and notary fees, and what the purchase process actually looks like.

There's no legal barrier to foreigners buying property in Germany — you don't need citizenship, permanent residency, or even to already live here. What actually determines whether you can buy, and on what terms, is your ability to finance the purchase.

Getting a mortgage as a newcomer

German banks are conservative lenders. They typically want a permanent employment contract, German residency, and a Schufa record (Germany's credit bureau) with enough history to assess you. If you've only recently relocated, expect banks to ask for a larger down payment — often 20–30% of the purchase price — until you've built up a longer German credit and income history.

The real cost of buying

The purchase price is only part of the bill. Grunderwerbsteuer — a one-time transfer tax set by each German state — ranges from 3.5% (Bavaria, Saxony) to 6.5% (several states including NRW and Brandenburg). On top of that, notary and land registry fees typically run 1.5–2%, and if you use a real estate agent, commission (often split between buyer and seller) adds several more percent.

The Notar process

Unlike in many countries, every German property purchase must be certified by a Notar — a neutral legal professional, not your own lawyer or the seller's. The Notar drafts the purchase contract, reads it aloud at signing, and handles registering you as the new owner in the Grundbuch (land registry). This adds a layer of legal protection but also means the process typically takes several weeks from signing to full completion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreigners buy property in Germany?
Yes. There are no legal restrictions on foreign nationals buying property in Germany, regardless of residency status or citizenship — the same process applies to residents and non-residents alike.
Can expats get a mortgage (Baufinanzierung) in Germany?
Yes, though banks generally want to see a permanent employment contract, German residency, a Schufa credit record, and often a higher down payment from newcomers — commonly 20–30% of the purchase price.
How much is Grunderwerbsteuer (real estate transfer tax)?
It ranges from 3.5% to 6.5% of the purchase price depending on the German state — for example 3.5% in Bavaria and Saxony, 6.5% in states like North Rhine-Westphalia and Brandenburg.
What does the Notar do and who pays?
A Notar is a neutral legal official required by law to certify every property purchase contract in Germany, and handles the land registry entry. Fees are typically 1.5–2% of the purchase price, usually paid by the buyer.
What are the total extra costs on top of the purchase price?
Budget roughly 10–15% on top of the purchase price for transfer tax, notary and land registry fees, and, if used, real estate agent commission.
